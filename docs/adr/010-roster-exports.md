# ADR 010: the roster's picture and sheet

Status: implemented. It adds to the roster of [ADR 008](008-roster-depth-chart.md), whose names stay on the device everywhere else.

## Why

A coach builds the roster on the field, a depth chart a position, and then has to get it to the people who need it: the assistant running the defense, the parent keeping the bench, the dugout wall. The panel answers "who plays X" one position at a time, and nothing could leave the device: rule 4 kept every name out of plays, links and printouts. Coaches asked for the roster itself as something to send and something to print, laid out the way they read a depth chart: where each kid lines up, who starts, who is next, and where the team is thin.

So the roster gets two exports of its own: a **picture** of the side on the field, sized for a phone, and a **sheet**, every position and every player, to print. Beside a device backup they are the only files with the names in them, and they are made only when the coach asks.

## Rules

1. **From the roster, only.** The roster panel's **EXPORT** group, under SPOTS, holds **Save roster picture** and **Download roster sheet PDF**, with a line saying what each is and that both carry the players' names. Play exports (cards, wristbands, binder, slides, flyer, postcards, a playbook file, a share link) still carry none (ADR 008, rules 3 and 4).
2. **The lineup on the field.** Both read the active lineup, as the field shows it: the Secondary Lineup made fresh from the starting lineup's charts and saying so, a branch saying what it was branched from. They list the positions the panel lists (`rosterPositions`): the standard formation where the coach has stood it, then every tag a lineup keeps a chart for, or a play wears, that has no token in it. They read and never write: nothing stored changes.
3. **The picture is the side on view.** The SHOW pills (and the header's badge) pick Offense, Defense or Both, and the picture follows: one side on the play card's 4:5 portrait, 1080 x 1350, both on a phone screen's 9:16, 1080 x 1920, so a card is the same size either way. It waits for a name: with nobody on any of the lineup's charts the button is off and the panel says why. A side with nobody on it says "Nobody is on this depth chart yet." and nothing about the app, since it is sent to someone who can't tap it.
4. **A depth board.** The formation is laid out on turf, defense above the LOS and offense below, each level of it a row of cards; a card hangs centred under the spot its position stands at across the field. On each card the token, the starter on the highlighter under the word "Starter", and the backups in order, "2nd" to "8th", their names lined up in one column. A position nobody is down for is a dashed card, "Unassigned"; a starter with nobody behind says "No backup" in ink as its 2nd line. The sides are named in ink either side of the line, in a lane no card enters, since red and blue print as one grey. Tags with no token are cards **Not in this formation** ("Positions with no spot in the formation."), their tokens dashed, on a shelf after the board, never on the turf; a crowd of them too big to sit beside the board is named in a line instead ("Not in this formation, on the sheet: …"). Over the foot, the gaps on view in a line ("Unassigned: Z · No backup: QB · No position: Riley"), and the footer says the team, the lineup and the day.
5. **The sheet is the whole roster, to print.** Letter or A4, as the device's language suggests (`defaultPaper`), at 220 dpi, each page laid out from its own size, its header inside a printer's margin. Page one is the board of both sides with every chart whole: on paper a position nobody is down for, or a starter with nobody behind, gets lines to pencil the missing names in. Then **Who plays where**: the **Gaps to fill** (positions unassigned, starters with no backup, players on no chart; one line when every position is open or nothing is missing), and every player, alphabetical, against every position, with where they play in words and their line on each chart, 1 on the highlighter for a starter. A column one deep or none says so in ink. With nobody on any chart the sheet is a blank depth chart and a blank grid to fill in by hand.
6. **Readable, or another page.** A name is wrapped at its spaces and hyphens and made smaller only down to a size that still reads, a word wider than its card broken at its letters, shared evenly over its lines; a starter is never smaller than a backup; a name too long for every line it is allowed loses its middle, never its end, which is what tells two long names apart. The picture cuts a long chart to "+n more on the sheet" before its words go under what a phone reads. The sheet never shrinks a name under its print size and never draws over its foot: the tags not in the formation go on pages of their own, then a side a page, a side too deep for one page going on over more a level of its formation at a time; the team goes on over more pages, and a side with more positions than a page holds at a readable width in another grid, each page with its header and "page n of N".
7. **Files.** `<team>-<lineup>-<side>.png` and `<team>-<lineup>-roster-sheet.pdf`, kebab-cased, "flag-football" for a team with no name; the PDF's title `<team> - <lineup> - roster sheet`. The picture is typed `image/png`, so a phone saves it as a picture. Every word is a real `<text>`, so the PDF keeps its words under the picture, as every PDF here does, and the sheet can be searched for a name.
8. **Said in the panel.** "Drawing the roster picture…", then "Saved", or "That export failed. Try again on a bigger screen." in a status line under the buttons (on a phone the panel is a drawer over the toast), the failure recorded with the device's diagnostics; the line stays where a screen reader hears it while it is empty. One export at a time, kept by the designer rather than the panel, so leaving roster mode mid-export neither loses its word nor starts a second.
9. **Colour.** The team's colour paints the band behind its name, ink or cream on it by contrast; the highlighter's yellow, which is also the colour of a team that never chose one, gives an ink band instead, since yellow means the starter.

## Data model

None. The exports are pure functions of what is stored (`lib/export/roster.ts`: `rosterPicture`, `rosterSheet`, `rosterFile`, `rosterTitle`), written down with their ways of going wrong first (E1–E17), and drawn by the export pipeline (`exportPng` and `exportPdf` in `lib/export/run.ts`), which lays the pages out only once the hand face has loaded. `rosterPositions` (`lib/play/lineup.ts`) is the list of positions roster mode's panel and the exports share.

## Out of scope

Jersey numbers, who is out this week, the opponent, and a parent's copy without the depth order: the roster stores none of the first three, and the last is a choice of what to leave out that the coach has not been offered yet.

## Verification

`e2e/journeys/depth-chart.journey.ts` drives it on all four device projects, with the clock and the time zone pinned: from roster mode on an offensive play the panel offers both exports with a line saying the names go with them; the picture saves for each side in turn as `riverside-otters-starting-lineup-<side>.png`, a PNG by its signature, header and trailer at 1080 x 1350 (1080 x 1920 for both), each its own file, the page it was drawn from naming every player on that side's charts (each a whole name, so "Jo" is never found in "Jordan") and nobody only on the other's, every chart on view card by card in its order ("No backup" as the 2nd line behind a lone starter), its heading, the lineup, "Unassigned" on Z's own card, the shelf and its explanation where FS is on view, the gaps on view and the day, and every word inside the picture's frame; the sheet saves as `riverside-otters-starting-lineup-roster-sheet.pdf`, two Letter pages titled `Riverside Otters - Starting Lineup - roster sheet`, its invisible words on their pages holding every chart whole on the board, card by card in its order, with lines to pencil in at Z and behind each lone starter, the shelf, and on the team page every player with where they play ("Riley No position", "Devon X starter, LC 2nd", Jo's own row) and the gaps to fill; the stored lineups are the same before and after; and a second sheet and picture are the same bytes. The Secondary Lineup's picture is named for it, says what it is made from, and has the next player up starting (Jo at X, Priya at Y, Owen at C, Aaliyah keeping QB with "No backup" as her 2nd line). A canvas the phone refuses says the failure once, in the panel, records it, saves nothing and gives the buttons back, and a picture saves once the canvas is back. An empty roster has its picture off and says why, and its sheet is a blank depth chart (a starter's and a backup's line at all ten positions) with "Every position is open: fill in the depth chart in pencil." and every player "No position"; with only the defense filled in, the offense's picture says "Nobody is on this depth chart yet." and nothing about the app. Each test leaves `test-results/depth-chart-<device>-*`: the three pictures, the sheet, a picture of each of its pages as drawn and a manifest (bytes and sha256 of each file, page and word counts, the browser, the clock), the Secondary Lineup's picture and words, and the empty roster's sheet and picture, uploaded as `depth-chart`.
