# ADR 010: the quarterback's rollout and boot

Status: implemented.

## Why

The designer moves the ball before a throw in several ways (a handoff, laterals, a pitch on the run), but the quarterback's own options were to throw from where they took the snap, keep it on a run, or toss it back. (A quarterback can also be given a receiver's route from the passing tree, but that carries the ball down a receiver's path and throws from wherever it ends up, past the line included; it is no quarterback action. A Pitch on the quarterback saved before [ADR 004](004-lateral-chains.md) reads back as a `throw` with a 1.5-yard step; it is kept as stored, below.) Two of the most-called flag plays move the quarterback first:

- **The rollout** (a sprint-out): the quarterback takes the snap and runs across behind the line toward a sideline, then throws on the run. It moves the launch point away from the rusher and puts half the field, and a flood of routes, in front of them.
- **The boot** (a bootleg): play-action the other way. The quarterback fakes the handoff to a runner going one way, then rolls out the opposite way to throw, after the defense has flowed with the fake. In 5v5 flag nobody blocks for them, so every boot is a naked boot.

Both are the quarterback's own jobs, so they get a group of their own in the palette.

## Rules

1. **Only the quarterback, at the snap.** Rollout and Boot are offered to the quarterback (the player labelled QB, else the default QB slot, as everywhere) for their time with the ball at the snap, in a **QB** group of their own, and to nobody else: not the centre, not a receiver, and not a carrier after a lateral. Picked for a time the quarterback tosses the ball on, one ends the chain there with that job, as a run picked there does. A rollout or boot on anyone else, or on a quarterback whose chain (after settling) has a lateral, is taken off when the play is read or edited.
2. **Both are passes.** The quarterback carries the ball to a **launch point** behind the line and throws from there to the read, as on any pass. Neither is a run, so neither is flagged in a no-run zone, and neither makes a play match Run in the playbook filter. Playback never turns one into a handoff by chance: with a rollout or boot the play is a run only when the call is (rule 8).
3. **The ball comes first.** The quarterback waits for the snap to reach them before moving. With a runner on the play they also fake, or hand off, **from where they took the snap**, where every run's exchange is laid out (beside a shotgun quarterback, in a jet's lane, under centre), and go only once the fake or the handoff is done.
4. **A rollout goes right unless mirrored.** It runs to the quarterback's right (the field's +x) wherever they line up; **⇄ Mirror route** sends it left, and **Flip play** flips it with the field.
5. **A boot goes the other way from the fake.** The fake is the read when the read is a run, else the runner who first comes within reach of the quarterback after the snap (a yard and a half: a jet sweep first of all, since it is already crossing; a Delay counts its wait), then any who never do, nearest first, ties going to the play's own order. The boot goes away from the side of the quarterback the fake's path is on after it passes them (its average across the field from its closest approach on), and right when that is dead level or there is no runner to fake to. A boot is never mirrored: it follows the fake, so moving the runner, changing their run or flipping the play turns it (all but flipping when the runner lines up on the quarterback's x, Q11). To go the same way as the fake, pick Rollout, mirrored when the fake goes left.
6. **The launch point is behind the line.** Every point of either path stays on the field, behind the line and in the backfield, and the launch point is at least `PITCH_SET` (2.6 yards) behind the line, so the throw is always a legal forward pass. Near a sideline the path is pulled in to fit, like every run's.
7. **The quarterback on a rollout or boot is never the read.** It can't be marked primary, and a read stored on one is taken off. Picking one over a quarterback's keeper that was the read leaves the play with no read.
8. **When the call is a run, the quarterback hands off and carries out the fake.** That is the read on a run, or a runner with nobody to throw to. The handoff goes to the fake at the quarterback's spot, then the quarterback runs the rollout or boot without the ball, as a decoy, ending in an arrow rather than the ring. It is flagged in a no-run zone like any run.

## Data model

`"rollout"` and `"boot"` are two new offensive route types: `{ type: "rollout" }`, `{ type: "rollout", mirror: true }` and `{ type: "boot" }` on the quarterback. Nothing else is stored, so they travel everywhere a route does (the draft, saved plays, share links, play and playbook files, backups, undo) through `normalizeRoute`, which accepts any type the route table knows. Stray fields on them from a file or link (a `mirror` on a boot, `pts`, a `target`) are kept and ignored, as a jet sweep's are.

- In the route table each has `end: "set"` (the ring the throw leaves from), `ball: "throw"` (a job that sets up and throws, so neither is in the passing tree or counts as a route to throw to) and `qb: true`. `isQbAction(type)` answers for both; `QB_KEYS` lists them.
- `rolloutLegs(qx, qy, side)` and `bootLegs(qx, qy, side)` in `lib/play/routes.ts` lay the paths out after the quarterback's spot (after any pre-snap motion), measured from their depth or 4 yards, whichever is deeper, so a quarterback under centre drops before rolling. The rollout opens and gains depth (2 across, 1.2 deeper), runs flat across (to 5.5) and turns up to the launch point 8 yards over and 0.8 in front of that depth. The boot pivots back on the fake's side (1 across, 1.6 deeper, clear of the token), bends behind the spot to the other side (3 across, 2 deeper), runs out (6 across, 1.4 deeper) and turns up to the same launch point on its side. Every point is clamped inside the sidelines and the backfield.
- `fakeOf(players)` and `qbSide(p, players)` in `lib/play/geometry.ts` answer rule 5 and which way the quarterback goes. Both read the fake's path from `routeYards` on the deepest card, whose shape near the quarterback does not depend on the card, and from where everyone stands at the snap, so the field, the pictures, the words and playback agree.
- `settleChain` takes a rollout or boot off anyone but the quarterback, and off the quarterback when the walked chain has a lateral, right after the jet sweep's rule and before any carrier rule (which would keep a ball job on the last carrier), and takes a read off one.
- `mirrorable` is true for a rollout and false for a boot; `flipRoute` toggles a rollout's mirror through `trimFlags`, so a play flipped twice stores as it did.
- A `throw` on the quarterback at the snap, which only a play saved with a Pitch before ADR 004 has, stays as stored and played: its step to the set depth and its set-up beat are unchanged. It is not offered, and picking Rollout or Boot replaces it.

## Playback

`planAfterSnap` lays the quarterback's track out from `routeYards` like any route and gives it a wait of `snapAt` (rule 3). With a rollout or boot the runner is always `fakeOf`, in teaching and simulation alike, and the run-or-pass coin flip simulation makes for an unmarked play-action is skipped. The runner's mesh, the handoff and a jet's lead are timed against the quarterback's spot exactly as on any play, since the quarterback is still standing there; the quarterback's track then holds at its start until `handAt + FAKE` on a pass or `handAt + handFor` on a run. The play's length is worked out again with that hold, so a decoy or a late fake never outlasts it. The throw leaves as the quarterback reaches the launch point (or later, if the route needs it), from where they are, so the ball rides in their hands until then and flies from the ring.

Ways it could go wrong, written down before the code:

- Q1 a rollout or boot offered to, or stored on, someone who is not the quarterback, or picked for a time other than the snap: never offered; the reducer refuses the pick before the branch that cuts a chain at that time; `settleChain` takes a stored one off.
- Q2 a rollout or boot on a quarterback whose chain has a lateral (QB → Z, QB → Z → QB, or Z's own after QB → Z): a carrier tosses it on or throws from where they caught it, so it is taken off ahead of the carrier rules, and a lateral picked over one replaces it (undo brings it back). A chain whose every lateral is cut keeps it.
- Q3 the quarterback leaves before the ball reaches them: their track waits `snapAt`.
- Q4 the quarterback rolls away from the mesh before the handoff or fake: their track holds at the spot until it is done, so the ball never chases a quarterback who already left, and a toss never stands in for a handoff.
- Q5 the boot's direction, the words and the fake in playback disagree: all three read `fakeOf`, from where everyone stands at the snap, and simulation never picks another runner or flips the call.
- Q6 a boot with no runner: it goes right, and the palette says there is nothing to fake yet.
- Q7 a fake whose path stays level with the quarterback after it passes them: the boot goes right.
- Q8 the launch point on or past the line: every point is held at least `LOS_GAP` behind the line and the launch point at least `PITCH_SET`.
- Q9 the path off the field: x is clamped inside the sidelines and y inside the backfield, so a quarterback near a sideline rolls a shorter way; points that collapse together are dropped from the track.
- Q10 a quarterback under centre, a yard behind the line: they fake there, then the path is measured from 4 yards deep, so they drop, then roll.
- Q11 Flip play on a rollout from the middle of the field: the side is not read off the spot (a quarterback at x = 15 would never flip), so `flipRoute` toggles the mirror. A boot flips with its fake, except when the fake lines up at the quarterback's x too: such a run goes the same way flipped (as it always has), so the boot does too; ⇄ Mirror on the run turns both.
- Q12 a read on the quarterback's rollout or boot: not offered, `togglePrimary` refuses it, and `settleChain` takes one off a stored rollout or boot.
- Q13 the call words: a rollout or boot with no runner is a pass; beside a runner it is play-action, the quarterback faking first; when the call is a run the quarterback is a decoy (rule 8). Nothing is flagged unless the call is a run. The call counts receivers as playback does, leaving out only `quarterback(players)` whatever anyone is tagged, so a second player tagged QB with a route is thrown to on the field, in the words and in playback alike.
- Q14 pre-snap motion: the path starts where the quarterback's motion ends, and the fake is found from where everyone stands at the snap, so a runner whose motion crosses another is ordered the same on the field and in playback.
- Q15 a jet sweep as the fake: the jet runner's lead runs first, the quarterback holds at the spot until the fake as they pass, then boots away from the sweep.
- Q16 no receivers and no runner: no call yet; the quarterback runs it with the ball and holds.
- Q17 two players labelled QB, or the quarterback renamed: only `quarterback(players)` is offered either, and the other has it taken off. Retyping the quarterback's tag can hand the job to the default QB slot mid-word; Undo brings the rollout back, as it does a lateral chain.
- Q18 the decoy, or a late fake (a Delay), outlasts the play: the play's length counts the quarterback's track with its hold, and the 5-second cap on everyone else's never cuts it short.
- Q19 two runners: the fake is the first to the quarterback, which Flip play does not change, so flipping turns the boot and keeps the fake.
- Q20 a Handoff fake, which is laid out from the runner's own spot and can cross behind the quarterback: its side is read from where its path goes after passing the quarterback, not where it ends.
- Q21 a runner who never comes within reach of the quarterback (a Handoff squeezed onto its spot deep in the backfield, or one that runs away): never the fake ahead of a back who meshes, and among themselves the nearest first.
- Q22 the palette's advice on turning a boot: it says Flip play only when flipping turns it, ⇄ Mirror on the fake's run when that does instead, and how to go with the fake (Rollout, mirrored when the fake goes left); a decoy is told to mark a receiver as the read, or to give one a route when there is nobody to throw to. Each is worked out on the play as it would be, never assumed.

## In the designer

- The **QB** group (accessible name "Quarterback": Rollout, Boot) comes first for the quarterback at the snap, under PRE-SNAP. A note under it says what the picked one does: which way they go and who they fake or hand off to, that ⇄ Mirror route turns a rollout, how to turn a boot or go with its fake (Q22), that there is nothing to fake yet, or, on a run, that they are a decoy and how to throw instead.
- On the field the path is drawn as a route is, ending in the ring the ball is thrown from, or in an arrow when the call is a run. **⇄ Mirror route** shows for a rollout. ★ Mark primary never shows for either.
- The field's description for a screen reader says what the quarterback does, in the call's words.
- The words follow the call and name the quarterback by their tag: "Pass: QB rolls out right and throws.", "Play-action: fake to Z, QB boots left, then throws." and "Run: Z takes it (Dive). QB hands off, then boots left as a decoy." on the call line; "Roll out right, throw, look to Y first", "Fake to Z, boot left, throw, look to Y first" and "Hand off to Z, then boot left as a decoy" as the quarterback's job, on slides, their alt text and speaker notes.

## Verification

`e2e/journeys/qb-actions.journey.ts` runs on all four device projects, with the page's random pinned so ▶ plays the plan the test builds. It draws a rollout to the read and checks the palette offers the QB group only to the quarterback, the drawn path stays behind the line and lands at the ring, playback holds the quarterback until the snap, carries the ball with them and throws from the ring, then mirrors it, shares it to a cleared device, flips it and reloads it. It draws a boot off a Dive fake and checks the path the other way, the fake at the spot before the boot, the throw from the far ring, simulation never handing it off unmarked, Flip play turning it, the ball on the 5 leaving it unflagged, the read moved onto the runner making it a flagged run with the quarterback as a decoy ending in an arrow, and a lateral replacing it with undo bringing it back. It boots off the first of two runners to the quarterback before and after Flip play, and from under centre. It downloads a book of a rollout, a boot and a boot on a run as slides and reads the call lines and jobs from the speaker notes. The phone-palette journey checks the quarterback's palette, QB group included, fits a phone. Each test leaves `test-results/qb-actions-<device>-<test>.json` and pictures, uploaded as `qb-actions`.
