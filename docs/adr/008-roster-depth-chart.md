# ADR 008: the roster, a depth chart by position

Status: implemented.

## Why

A play names its players by position: C, QB, X, Y and Z on offense, LC, LB, R, RC and S on defense, the tags the wristbands, slides and binder pages count by. A coach knows those positions by the kids who play them, and wanted the field to say so: who X is, and who steps in at X when that kid is away. A tag is three letters wide, so a name can't go in it, and a name on a play would be stored in every play, every share link and every printout, where a child's name has no business going. So the roster is its own thing: a mode that lays the positions out on the field, with a depth chart per position that the whole library reads from.

## Rules

1. **A position is a tag.** The roster keys its slots by team and tag, exactly as a player's label is stored. Whoever wears X on any play is played by X's starter; two players wearing the same tag on one play share the slot (R5). A tag renamed on a play is a fresh position with nobody down for it (R1); the old slot keeps its names for whoever wears that tag next, and undoing the rename brings them back. A player with no tag has no slot (R6).
2. **Starter first.** Each slot is a list of names, the starter at the top; **▲** moves a line up, **✕** takes it off, **Add a player** puts a blank line at the bottom, up to eight (`MAX_DEPTH`). A blank line is kept as typed while the coach fills it in (R2): nothing is trimmed under their fingers. A slot with no lines left is dropped (R3), so an empty depth chart stores nothing.
3. **The starter's name goes under the tag on the field**, in roster mode and while drawing the play, shortened to fit (R4): whole when it is twelve letters or fewer, else the first word, else cut with an ellipsis. The panel keeps the whole name. A position nobody is down for shows no caption on a play; in roster mode it reads "Unassigned" (or "No tag"), so the gaps are plain.
4. **The roster is the team's, on this device.** It is written as it is typed, like the team's name, never undone, and goes into a device backup and comes back from one. It is never written into a play, a share link, a playbook file, a thumbnail or a printout: the names stay with the coach.
5. **Lineups are versions of the roster.** The **Starting Lineup** is always there and can't be deleted. Any lineup can be **branched**: the branch starts as a copy of the lineup it came from (a branch of a branch copies the branch, V4), is active at once, is named "Lineup n" until the coach names it, and says what it was branched from. The field, every play and every caption follow the **active** lineup, and the pick outlasts a reload. Deleting a lineup asks once, in place (it is not undoable); the field falls back to the lineup it was branched from, else the starting lineup (V2), and its own branches forget it (V3). Up to twenty lineups.
6. **Every name has a page.** The panel lists everyone on the active lineup, each name once however it was typed (trimmed, case aside, R7), and a name opens that player's page: every position they are down for on this lineup and where they sit at each ("X · Starter", "LC · 2nd"). A position on the page opens its depth chart; tapping the field leaves the page.
7. **Roster mode is the formation alone.** Routes, arcs, man tags, the no-run stamp, waypoints and the ▶ button are off; both teams are on the field, the play's own side in front and the other faded, every position tappable; dragging still lines players up, and that is the play's edit, undoable as ever. Leaving roster mode brings the play back as it was.

## Data model

`ffpd.lineups.v1` holds `{ active, lineups }`: each lineup `{ id, name, from?, slots }`, `from` the id of the lineup it was branched from, and `slots` a roster, a list of `{ team, tag, depth }`: `tag` up to three letters as a label is stored, `depth` the names, starter first, each up to forty letters with control characters out. `normalizeRoster` (lib/play/storage.ts) drops a slot with no tag or no lines, ignores a second slot for the same tag on the same team, and caps the list at thirty slots and each slot at eight lines; `normalizeLineups` puts the starting lineup back (empty, at the front) when it is missing, ignores a second lineup with an id already seen, drops a `from` that names no lineup, makes the starting lineup active when the active id names none, and caps the set at twenty; `readLineups` answers the starting lineup alone, empty, for a device that has none. The library store (lib/play/library.ts) serves them with `getLineups` / `setLineups` beside the team, so every screen re-renders on a write and nothing is reported kept until it reads back. The pure helpers are in `lib/play/roster.ts` (`slotOf`, `depthOf`, `starterOf`, `withDepth`, `shortName`, `starters`, `depthWord`, `playerNames`, `standings`) and `lib/play/lineup.ts` (`activeLineup`, `withActive`, `withSlots`, `renamed`, `branched`, `without`, `fromName`), each with its ways of going wrong written down first (R1–R7, V1–V5).

The device backup (`lib/export/backup.ts`) carries `lineups`; a backup from before this change has none and restores the empty starting lineup, so version 1 stands. A playbook file does not carry them.

## In the designer

- **Roster** is a tile in Play tools, under its own ROSTER heading before TEAM. It toggles the mode; the header's right toggle reads **Roster** instead of **Routes** and opens the roster panel in the same sidebar; **Clear** waits for the play. The field's title reads "Roster" with the team's name under it.
- The roster panel opens on the LINEUP: a picker of every lineup, the active one's name to edit, "Branched from …" when it was, **Branch lineup** and, off the starting lineup, **Delete lineup**, which turns into "Delete …?" with **Delete** and **Keep**. The field's title reads "Roster" with the active lineup's name under it.
- Then the position tapped: its tag field (the same one the route palette has) and heading "Who plays X", the DEPTH CHART with a numbered line per name, the starter's number on the highlighter, and a note saying where the names go and don't. Under it, PLAYERS, everyone on the lineup with where they sit, each a button to that player's page; then every position on both sides, the play's side first, left to right as the wristbands count them, each a button that selects that player and says who starts and how deep ("X: Devon, 2 deep"); and **‹ Back to routes**.
- Entering roster mode leaves whatever was being drawn, keeps the selection (so a player picked in the palette is the position whose chart opens) and opens the panel.
- A screen reader hears "Played by Devon" on a token that has a starter.

## Verification

`e2e/journeys/roster.journey.ts` drives it on all four device projects: roster mode hides the routes and the ▶ button and lists all ten positions as unassigned; X takes three names, the second is moved up to start and the third taken off, the field's caption following each step; the list is the way to the quarterback, whose long name is shortened under the token and whole in the list; the stored lineups are exactly the starting lineup with the two slots; Devon's page lists X · Starter and LC · 2nd (typed "devon" at LC) and its X is the way to X's chart; back in the play the routes return and the names stay, only where somebody is down; the roster outlasts a reload; a share link and its page carry no name, and opening it back in the designer shows the names again; a defensive call lists the defenders first; a tag renamed reads unassigned and undo brings the names back; a device backup carries the lineups and restoring it on a wiped device brings the captions back; a write the device refuses is said once and stores nothing; and lineups branch from the starting lineup and from each other as copies, the field follows the one picked, the pick outlasts a reload, and a deleted lineup asks once, falls back to its parent and is forgotten by its branches. Each test leaves `test-results/roster-<device>*.json` with pictures of the panel, the field, a player's page and the lineups beside it, uploaded as `roster`.
